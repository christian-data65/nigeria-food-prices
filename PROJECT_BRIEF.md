# PROJECT BRIEF
## The Cost of Eating in Nigeria — A Real-World Data Analytics Project

### 1. Project Overview

This project analyzes food prices in Nigeria using public data from different sources. The aim is to understand how food prices change over time, how prices differ across states, and how exchange rates and inflation relate to food prices.

The project is designed to support better purchasing, sales, and business decisions for Ọja Foods.

### 2. Stakeholders and Their Needs

| Stakeholder | Information Needed |

| Sales Team | States where food prices are high or increasing, to support sales decisions. |

### 3. Business Questions

1. Which staple foods had the highest price increases and decreases over the last 19 months?
2. Which states currently have the cheapest and most expensive prices for each selected food item?
3. Is there a difference between food prices in Northern and Southern Nigeria?
4. Which food items have the biggest price differences between two states?
5. Does the naira exchange rate affect imported rice prices more than local rice prices?
6. Are there seasonal patterns in food prices, and which months usually have cheaper prices?
7. How different are official NBS food prices from World Bank crowdsourced market prices?
8. How much does a basic food basket for a family of five cost in each state compared with the national minimum wage?
9. What are the expected prices of three selected staple foods over the next three months?

### 4. Data Sources

The project brief describes four main dataset themes, while its data source table lists these five source categories:

1. **NBS Food Price Watch:** Monthly average prices of selected food items across Nigerian states.
2. **World Bank Monthly Food Price Estimates (RTFP):** Monthly food prices from markets across Nigeria.
3. **High Frequency Crowdsourced Prices (HFCP):** Daily food prices collected from markets across Nigeria.
4. **CBN Exchange Rates:** Official daily naira exchange rates.
5. **NBS CPI and Inflation Reports:** Monthly inflation statistics, including food inflation.

Each source will first be inspected and analyzed separately. Suitable data will be integrated later for questions that require comparisons between sources.
